# Dr. Prashant Agrawal — Tone of Voice

## Who is speaking

A practising dermatologist of 17+ years who treats around 1.5 lakh patients' worth of
real skin, runs Dermadent in Udaipur, and then goes into a lab and formulates the
product he could not find for them. He co-founded SkinInspired with Piyush Jain.

The distinction that drives everything: **he is not a doctor endorsing a product. He
is the doctor who made it.** His authority comes from the clinic, and his credibility
comes from proving things on his own skin rather than asserting them.

**One line:** *Calm clinic authority plus maker's pride. He names the condition,
explains why the category failed it, says "so I formulated this", and then proves it
instead of claiming it.*

## The five moves he actually makes

Drawn from his performed scripts (Manila sunscreen field tests, ACNIL-3, iREVIVE,
the USM derma films). Evidence in `references/corpus.md`.

**1. Open from the clinic.**
> "As a dermatologist, I deal with hundreds of patients every day, who say…"
> "I see patients every week with this exact condition. Almost none of them know its name."

**2. Name the condition.** This is his signature and nobody else in the category can
do it. He uses the medical term, then immediately translates it.
> "The darkening in your underarms is Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation, caused by
> friction, shaving trauma, and chemical irritants."

**3. Take responsibility as the maker.** First person, active, proud.
> "So, I decided to create a solution."
> "I didn't just formulate a sunscreen. I formulated one you'd actually use twice."
> "I built the sunscreen I couldn't find."
> "That is why I formulated the UnderArm Serum Mist."

**4. Prove it, don't claim it.** He runs experiments on himself: one arm treated and
one bare, 11+ UV index, 85% humidity, swim, tissue blot, before and after. Then he
delivers the verdict in fragments.
> "It stayed." · "Still. There." · "Mine is." · "The difference is clear."

**5. Close warm, on the person.**
> "We want to treat your acne more gently than you do."
> "Because your skin deserves it."

## Structure of a Dr. Prashant reel

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | A clinic line, a named condition, or a self-experiment that sounds slightly reckless for a doctor ("I'm about to deliberately damage my own skin. On camera."). |
| **Clinic evidence** | What patients actually say, what he actually sees. |
| **Category failure** | Why the existing product was never built for this. Aimed at the product category, never at the person using it. |
| **"So I formulated…"** | The maker's turn. Names the product once. |
| **Mechanism** | Actives mapped to the condition, or a plain-life analogy. |
| **Demonstration** | The test, or the correct way to use it, shown on camera. |
| **Spec line** | Flat, factual: "SPF 80+. PA++++. No Oxybenzone. Water resistant." |
| **Warm close** | One line back to the person. |

## Diction and texture

- **Calm and unhurried.** His script directions literally read *calm tone, subtle
  smile, reassuring, warm.* He never raises his voice to make a point.
- **English-led, Hinglish when relaxed.** Clinic and mechanism in English; the warm
  and playful beats in Hindi: *"Hi friends!"*, *"Doston,"*, *"Aaj main Manila mein
  hoon"*, *"challenge accepted!"*. On-camera address: friends, doston, you guys.
- **Fragments only after proof.** "It stayed." lands because a full demonstration
  came before it. Fragments without evidence just sound like ad copy.
- **Numbers as specification, never as excitement.** SPF 80+, PA++++, 11+ UV index,
  90% humidity, 12.10% Triple AHA Complex. Stated flatly, then moved on from.
- **Analogy for anything complicated.** Dual-chamber became *"you wouldn't mix your
  pre-workout the night before"*. PDRN, exosomes and peptides became architect,
  material and labour building a home. Reach for the everyday object first.
- **Second person, gentle.** "your skin", "aapki skin". He advises, he doesn't instruct.
- **Rhetorical question he then answers.** "Your sunscreen is sweating with you right
  now. The question is, is it still working? Mine is."

## Never

- **Never blame the person.** This is the hard rule, and his own writing models the
  fix: *"When do you stop and rub sunscreen all over your body? You don't. And I get
  it, cream sunscreen was never built for that moment."* The failure belongs to the
  product, always.
- **Never name or compare competitors.** He describes what the category does wrong.
  Brand names never enter it.
- **Never oversell.** No "miracle", no "transform your skin overnight", no superlatives
  about his own formula beyond what a test shows.
- **Never lead with his credentials.** They appear as a name super or a passing line,
  not as the pitch. The condition leads, then the product, then the doctor.
- **Never go cold and clinical.** Jargon appears only when he immediately translates it.
  He is an approachable expert, not a lecturer.
- **Never coach or moralise.** No habit lectures, no "you should have known".
- **Never fear-monger.** Real clinical consequence, stated evenly, is enough.

## Claims discipline

He can say something on camera only if one of these is true: it is in the product's
approved claim list, it is visible in the demonstration he just filmed, or it is a
standard dermatological fact about the condition. When a claim needs a qualifier,
he keeps it: *helps reduce*, *with regular use*, *up to 48 hours*, *in a consumer study*.

Two habits from his older scripts to watch, because they collide with SkinInspired's
positioning rules:

1. He sometimes opens a product section with an ingredient percentage list. Name the
   condition first, then the actives as the answer to it.
2. He has used fast-result lines like "clearing acne in less than three days" and
   "visible difference from the very first use". Keep these only where the claim is
   substantiated and approved, and keep the qualifier attached.

## Pairing him with a creator

When he shares a reel with a business or beauty creator, the split is fixed:

- **He gets** the clinic observation, the condition, the formulation reasoning, the
  usage demonstration, and the claims. Claims in a creator's mouth sound like a pitch.
  In his, they sound like a diagnosis.
- **He never gets** the sales push, the award, or the "go buy it" line. Third-party
  credibility said by the founder becomes bragging, so let the other person say it.
- **He answers, he doesn't present.** Being asked a question is his most natural
  posture on camera, which is why interview and "Ask the derm" formats suit him.
- His closing beat is the partnership line, not a CTA: the product's job is done, the
  person's job is to use it daily.

## Worked example

**Brief:** 30s derm-to-camera on underarm pigmentation.

> **Hook:** I see patients every week with this exact condition. Almost none of them
> know its name.
>
> **Condition:** Underarm darkening is Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation. Friction,
> shaving trauma, and the alcohol and fragrance in a regular deodorant keep irritating
> that skin, and irritated skin makes more melanin.
>
> **Category failure:** Wo skin ki problem hai. Deodorant usse cover karta hai, theek
> nahi karta. Underarm bhi skin hai.
>
> **Maker's turn:** It responds to the same actives I use for facial PIH. Nobody was
> delivering those to the underarm, so I formulated the UnderArm Serum Mist.
>
> **Usage:** Clean and dry underarm, 10 to 15 cm away, 2 to 3 sprays, let it absorb.
> Waxed today? Skip today, spray tomorrow.
>
> **Close:** Aapki underarm skin bhi utni hi attention deserve karti hai jitni aapka
> chehra. 🙂

## Self-check before delivering

- [ ] Does it open from the clinic or from a named condition?
- [ ] Is there a "so I formulated this" moment in first person?
- [ ] Is the failure pinned on the category, never on the viewer?
- [ ] Is every claim approved, demonstrated, or a standard clinical fact, with its
      qualifier intact?
- [ ] Is the jargon translated within one sentence?
- [ ] Is he proving something rather than asserting it?
- [ ] Does it close warm, on the person, without a hard CTA?
